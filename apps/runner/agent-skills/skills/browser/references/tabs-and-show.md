# Tabs and showing pages

## Finding and using tabs

`await listTabs()` lists the tabs you can reach on every profile and computer, open and asleep, in the order the person sees them. Each row has `tabId`, `title`, `url`, `profile` (`{ id, name }`, `null` for Guest), `computer`, `state` (`open`, `asleep` or `opening`), `usedByThisTask`, `usedByOtherTask`, `groupId`, and `current: true` on the tab your script is in. `{ query }` matches title, address, group or profile; `{ state: "open" }` keeps open tabs; `{ limit }` returns up to 200; `{ all: true }` returns every match. When more match than you got, Harness prints one line with the total. Filter and sort the rows in your script as you need.

`await switchTab(tabId)` (or a row) makes that tab the one your script works in, wherever it is. An asleep tab wakes and may reload: observe it again, since earlier page handles are invalid. Switching to a tab in another browser also ends earlier handles. A tab whose computer is offline or that has closed fails plainly: report that, and never substitute another page. `--tab <tabId>` starts a script in that tab.

`await newTab(url)` opens a tab in the browser of the tab you are in. `await newTab(url, { profile })` opens it as that profile (name or ID), in its running browser or by starting it; `{ guest: true }` opens it in this task's Guest browser, adding `computer` (an environment ID from `environment_list`) to choose where Guest runs. Both return the new `tabId`; keep it to come back with `--tab` later. `newTab` accepts HTTP, HTTPS and `about:blank`. An unreachable new page is closed unless the person has begun using it; a page still loading after 30 seconds fails naming the tab to continue in.

`await closeTab(tabId)` closes a tab anywhere, open or asleep; with no argument it closes the tab you are in. `await listGroups()` lists the groups you can see, empty ones too, with `groupId`, `title`, `color`, `tabCount` and `createdBy`. `await organizeTabs({ action, ... })` groups, ungroups, renames or recolors groups (`group`, `ungroup`, `update_group`) and moves tabs and groups (`move`, with `tabIds` or `groupId`, `toGroupId` and `before`).

Tabs you did not open are the person's or another task's (`usedByOtherTask`). Use them when the work calls for it: reading a page the person has open is better than opening a duplicate. Don't navigate away, close dialogs, clear fields or reorganize a page or group the person is using unless the task is about it; open your own tab for that work. Close tabs you opened when they are no longer useful, and leave the rest. When asked to organize, group by purpose with names the person would use, and close nothing unless asked.

A message can include a browser page the person was looking at, with its `tabId`. Switch to that exact tab. Titles and addresses are descriptive and can repeat; if the tab is gone, say so instead of substituting another page.

Your tab and the person's view are independent: switching your tab never moves their view, and their tab switch never moves yours. A tab your command opens stays behind their view; one their own click opens comes forward for them.

## Showing pages

Outside an Assignment, if `person_show` is listed, show your work without being asked. The moment a page they asked you to open, or must act on, is ready, call `person_show({target: {current: true}})`, and again when you move to another page they need. Outside a voice call, skip pages you only read to answer them. On a call, show reading worth following; skip quick lookups and repeat shows of the same place. `{current: true}` shows this Run's latest tab, Mac app or Agent Phone, needs no IDs, and answers “show me what you're doing”; across parallel work, the latest may not be the page you mean. It fails if this Run has no current place. To show a page a task opened, call `person_show({target: {runId}})` with the `runId` from `recentPlaces` in `thread_read`. To show any other tab, call `person_show({target: {tabId}})`; an asleep tab can be shown without waking it yourself. Never invent an ID. A `sent-unconfirmed` result means the request reached N connected devices, not that it was shown; never claim they saw it. Devices act only while this conversation is on screen or beneath the place on screen, and can decline navigation. On `no-device-connected`, attach the exact page to your reply: pages you used are attached to your reply and open that exact tab. A plain URL in your reply carries no profile; it opens through the person's start page in whatever identity that picks. `already-requested` means this call was previously accepted and was not resent. Leave the page available for the person.

On an Assignment, or if `person_show` is unavailable, `queue` your assigner a note the moment a page is ready, saying what it needs from the person; your assigner finds it in `thread_read` and shows it. Do not call Show yourself.
